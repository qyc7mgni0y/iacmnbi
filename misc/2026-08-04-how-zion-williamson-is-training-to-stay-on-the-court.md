Zion Williamson is the most-hyped young basketball player since LeBron James, largely due to his incomparable physical gifts and highlight reel dunks. The 20-year-old phenom stands 6'6'' and weighs 284 pounds (at least according to his current player bio for the New Orleans Pelicans), and his sheer mass combined with an absurd level of athleticism makes him an anomaly on courts filled with slimmer players. He's the type of once-in-a-generation talent that shapes the fates of pro franchises and fronts magazine covers (like the latest issue of Men's Health, for instance).

While that speed, size, and power has led to success—he averaged 22.5 points per game as a rookie in the 2019-20 NBA season—Williamson has also faced a number of injuries in his young career. His recovery from a preseason knee surgery meant he only appeared in 24 games in that pandemic-shortened rookie year, and the most notable moment in his single, remarkable college season was when Williamson blew out his shoe and sprained his knee in a nationally televised rivalry game.

Naturally, the organizations who have bet on Williamson fulfilling his potential are eager to see him complete a season injury-free. Griffin set forth two essential fitness goals for Williamson that typically don't apply to basketball prospects, who usually strive to pack muscle onto spare, gangly frames in their early pro years: “Flexibility and putting himself in a position where he can control all that torque.”

Details about Williamson's exact training protocol are scarce, but when the Pelicans shared an Instagram post in July to highlight players working out before the NBA season restarted in Orlando, the results looked promising. Williamson, who might have been described as 'beefy' before, looked to be composed of muscle hewn from granite.

According to Williamson, the muscles were thanks to basic workout standards. “Believe it or not, pushups and situps,” he says. “And a lot of running.”

But he did more than just a few bodyweight drills to get fit. Williamson revealed that his daily exercise regimen also consisted of two sessions on the basketball court, plus flexibility, strength, and conditioning work away from the court with a trainer.
